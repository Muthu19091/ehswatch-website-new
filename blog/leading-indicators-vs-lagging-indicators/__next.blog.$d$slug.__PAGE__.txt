4:T694,<p>Walk into ten EHS team rooms and nine of them have a dashboard counting incidents, lost-time days and recordables. Those numbers matter — but they are all <strong>lagging indicators</strong>. By the time the chart moves, someone has already been hurt.</p><p>The teams genuinely pulling incident rates down focus their leadership attention on <strong>leading indicators</strong>: near-miss reports per worker, safety observation rate, training currency, action close-out speed, audit finding ageing. These metrics shift weeks or months before the lagging ones do — they tell you what is <em>going</em> to happen, not what already did.</p><h3>Three leading indicators every dashboard should have</h3><p><strong>1. Observations per worker per month.</strong> A worker logging one positive or at-risk observation a month is engaged with safety. A workforce averaging below 0.3 is not. This metric correlates more strongly with incident rate than any audit score we have measured.</p><p><strong>2. Action close-out cycle time.</strong> Median days from finding raised to action verified-closed. When this drifts above 30 days, your actions are not actions — they are wishes.</p><p><strong>3. Training currency.</strong> Percentage of workers whose certifications are current for the roles they are performing today. Anything below 95% is an audit finding waiting to happen.</p><h3>How to make the switch</h3><p>Add the leading indicators to your existing dashboard <em>alongside</em> the lagging ones — do not replace, augment. Set targets for the leading metrics first; the lagging ones will follow.
